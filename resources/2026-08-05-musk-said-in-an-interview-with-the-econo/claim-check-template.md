# Claim-check template for cross-domain mortality comparisons

Use this template when comparing deaths from different causes, countries, or datasets.

## 1. State the claim

**Claim:**

> [Insert the exact claim here.]

## 2. Identify the numbers

| Number | What it measures | Source | Geography | Time period | Counted or modeled? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Example: 61,672] | [Example: heat-related deaths] | [Example: Nature Medicine] | [Example: Europe] | [Example: summer 2022] | [Example: modeled] |
| [Example: 44,447] | [Example: firearm deaths] | [Example: Pew / CDC] | [Example: United States] | [Example: 2024] | [Example: counted] |

## 3. Check comparability

Ask:

- Are the geographies the same size?
- Are the years the same?
- Are the populations similar in size and age structure?
- Is one figure modeled while the other is directly counted?
- Are both measuring deaths, or is one measuring excess deaths, attributable deaths, incidents, or hospitalizations?
- Are indirect deaths included?
- Are uncertainty intervals provided?

## 4. Convert to rates when useful

Formula:

```text
rate per 100,000 people = deaths / population * 100,000
```

Example structure:

```text
61,672 deaths / [European population denominator] * 100,000 = rate per 100,000
44,447 deaths / [U.S. population denominator] * 100,000 = rate per 100,000
```

Do not mix population denominators casually. If the heat study covers 35 European countries, use the population of those countries, not all of Europe unless the source does.

## 5. Add the uncertainty

For modeled estimates, look for a confidence interval or credible interval.

Example wording:

> The heat-death figure is an estimate and should be reported with its uncertainty interval when available.

## 6. Separate factual comparison from policy conclusion

A factual comparison might be:

> The reported heat-death estimate for Europe in summer 2022 is numerically larger than the reported U.S. firearm-death count for 2024.

A policy conclusion requires more evidence:

> Local funding decisions should consider local risk, intervention effectiveness, cost, equity, and urgency.

## 7. Safer final wording

Use this structure:

> [Source A] estimated [number] deaths from [cause A] in [place/time]. [Source B] reported [number] deaths from [cause B] in [place/time]. The comparison is imperfect because [main caveat]. Still, it shows [limited, defensible takeaway].

## 8. Red flags

Be cautious if a post:

- compares one bad year with an average year
- compares a continent with a country without saying so
- ignores population size
- treats modeled estimates as exact counts
- treats death-certificate counts as perfectly complete
- skips uncertainty intervals
- turns a public-health comparison into a false either/or funding choice
