# Idea-Judgment Prompts

Copy-paste prompts to help you make the one decision AI can't make for you: *what's worth building*. Use these with any capable LLM.

---

## 1. Pressure-Test an Idea

```
You are a skeptical early-stage investor. I will describe an idea.
Do NOT encourage me. Your job is to find the fastest way it could fail.

Idea: [describe in 2-3 sentences]

Respond with:
1. The single weakest assumption I'm making.
2. Who specifically feels this pain, and evidence I'd need to confirm they exist.
3. What people currently do instead (the real competitor).
4. The cheapest test I could run this weekend to validate or kill it.
5. A blunt verdict: worth a Saturday, or not yet — and why.
```

---

## 2. Find the Real Problem Behind a Feature Idea

```
I want to build: [feature/product].
Act as a product coach using "5 Whys." Ask me one why-question at a time,
wait for my answer, and keep going until we reach the underlying human problem.
Then tell me whether the problem is worth solving and why.
```

---

## 3. Define Kill Criteria Before You Start

```
Help me set honest kill criteria for this project so I don't fool myself later.

Project: [describe]
Time budget: [e.g. one weekend]
Who I'll show it to: [audience]

Give me:
- 3 measurable signals that mean CONTINUE
- 3 measurable signals that mean KILL
Make them observable within one week and impossible to rationalize away.
```

---

## 4. Prediction Journal Entry (build judgment)

```
Before I launch, interview me to capture my predictions.
Ask me: expected number of users, expected top complaint,
expected reason it might flop, and my confidence (0-100%) for each.
Summarize my answers as a dated journal entry I can compare against reality later.
```

---

## 5. Post-Mortem / Post-Win Review

```
I just [shipped / killed] a project. Here's what happened: [facts + numbers].
Here's what I predicted beforehand: [paste prediction journal].

Compare prediction vs reality. Tell me:
1. Where my judgment was accurate.
2. Where I was systematically wrong (and the likely bias behind it).
3. One rule I should carry into the next idea.
```

---

**Usage tip:** The value isn't the AI's answer — it's forcing yourself to answer first. Write your own take before reading the model's. The gap between the two is where judgment grows.
