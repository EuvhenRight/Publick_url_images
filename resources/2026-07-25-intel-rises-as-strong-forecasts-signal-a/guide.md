# Reading Earnings Beyond the Headline: The Intel Q2 2025 Case

This guide teaches you to decompose an earnings 'swing' into its real drivers, using Intel's reported turnaround as a worked example. The goal: never let a headline like 'AI boost' substitute for the income statement.

## 1. The trap: headline attribution vs. actual cause

A headline attributes a stock move to a single narrative (here, 'AI boost'). But a profit swing has multiple components. Separate them:

| Driver | Nature | Recurring? |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Absence of prior-year impairments | Accounting / one-time | No |
| Operating cost cuts (R&D, SG&A) | Structural but finite | Partly |
| Segment revenue growth | Organic demand | Yes (if durable) |
| Forward guidance / node commitments | Expectation-driven | N/A (future) |

## 2. Worked decomposition (illustrative numbers from the post)

The post cites a ~$20B swing from loss to a ~$4.1B result. Key facts stated:
- Prior year included **$15.9B in impairments** that did not repeat.
- **~20% cut** to R&D and admin costs.
- **Data Center & AI segment slipped 1%** — i.e., the 'AI' narrative did NOT drive the swing.

So the profit came mostly from things NOT repeating plus cost discipline — not from selling more AI chips. That is a real recovery, but a *different kind* than the headline implies.

### Quick mental model
```
Profit swing ≈ (impairments that didn't repeat)
             + (cost reductions)
             + (organic segment change)
             ± (other one-offs)
```
If the first two terms dominate, the swing is a **cleanup**, not **growth**.

## 3. The real signal: the 14A commitment

The more forward-looking item is Intel committing to high-volume **14A** production in 2028 — a node it had threatened to abandon absent customer demand. This is an *expectations* rerating: the market prices in who might arrive in three years, not what shipped last quarter.

When evaluating a distant-forecast rerating, ask:
1. **What has to be true** for the forecast to pay off? (Foundry customers commit volume.)
2. **Who bears the risk** if it doesn't? (Capex is spent now; revenue is 2028+.)
3. **Is there external validation** or only management assertion? (Cited: customer demand changed the CEO's mind.)

## 4. Checklist for any 'turnaround on strong forecast' story

- [ ] Split the reported profit into recurring vs. one-time components.
- [ ] Check whether the headlined segment actually grew.
- [ ] Identify how much of the swing is cost-cutting (finite) vs. demand (scalable).
- [ ] For forward guidance, name the concrete milestone and its date.
- [ ] Ask who is putting capital at risk on both sides of the bet.
- [ ] Distinguish 'the business improved' from 'the story improved.'
