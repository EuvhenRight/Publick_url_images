# The Hype-to-Work Gap: A Field Guide for Reading Robotics (and Any Emerging-Tech) Claims

This guide turns the LinkedIn post's core insight into a reusable analysis framework. The central idea: **a hyped technology claim is often doing double duty** — it describes a real capability *and* projects a future that hasn't happened yet. Your job as an analyst, engineer, or buyer is to separate the two.

## The Three Gaps

Every impressive claim can be walked forward to the moment it becomes actual, paid, unsupervised work. Along the way, three gaps repeatedly appear:

### 1. Shipping isn't Deployed
A product can be "ready to ship" long before anyone runs a real workload on it.
- **Signal to look for:** ship date vs. first-real-use date.
- **Example:** Atlas is announced as "ready to ship" in 2026, but the parts-sequencing job doesn't begin until 2028 — a two-year gap.

### 2. Deployed isn't Collaborating
A unit can be physically present on a floor while still being fenced off from people.
- **Signal to look for:** barriers, safety cages, teleoperation, human minders.
- **Example:** Most humanoids stand behind barriers because they still fall over — which quietly cancels any "works alongside people" pitch.

### 3. The First Customer is the Maker
Early "deployments" are frequently the manufacturer using its own product in its own facility.
- **Signal to look for:** is the customer independent, or is it the vendor's own factory?
- **Example:** XPENG's IRON went into mass production for XPENG's own factories; Tesla's Optimus targets its own Fremont line.

## Level vs. Slope

The post's sharpest point: **the slope keeps getting read as if it were the level.**
- **Level:** ~16,000 humanoids installed worldwide in 2025 — tiny against any real workforce.
- **Slope:** the growth rate, which is what actually makes people nervous.

When you read a market chart, ask: is this describing *today's installed base* or *a projected trajectory*? A glossy chart of the future wearing a press release is a forecast, not a fact.

## A Five-Question Checklist

Apply to any hyped-technology announcement:

1. **Ship vs. use:** What is the ship date, and what is the first *real-use* date? How big is the gap?
2. **Autonomy:** Is it working unsupervised, or behind barriers / teleoperated / babysat?
3. **Customer independence:** Is the first customer independent of the maker?
4. **Level vs. slope:** Is the cited number an installed base (level) or a growth rate / forecast (slope)?
5. **Double duty:** Does this claim describe present capability, project a future, or both?

## Scoring

Give each answer a Readiness point:
- +1 if ship==use (no gap)
- +1 if fully autonomous alongside people
- +1 if the first customer is independent
- +1 if the number is a real installed base, not a forecast
- +1 if the claim is purely descriptive, not aspirational

**0–2:** Mostly forecast. Treat as directional, not deliverable.
**3–4:** Mixed. Real progress with meaningful caveats.
**5:** Genuinely deployed reality.

## Why This Matters Beyond Robots

The same three gaps apply to AI agents, self-driving cars, quantum computing, and most "revolutionary" B2B tools: demoed isn't deployed, deployed isn't autonomous, and the first reference customer is often an insider. Use the checklist before you budget, staff, or forecast around a claim.
